---
title: "Transition Planning"
description: "Questions for transition planning and post-school goals."
---

Transition planning should help the IEP team connect school support to life after high school. The plan should be specific enough that the family and student can understand the path.

<Info>
  IDEA requires transition services in the IEP no later than the first IEP in effect when the child turns 16, or younger if the IEP team determines it is appropriate. Illinois families should verify current state guidance and local practice with ISBE or the school team.
</Info>

## What to review

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Student voice" icon="user-round" horizontal>
    Strengths, preferences, interests, support needs, and what the student wants life after school to look like.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Postsecondary goals" icon="flag" horizontal>
    Goals related to education, training, employment, and independent living when appropriate.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Transition assessments" icon="clipboard-check" horizontal>
    Age-appropriate information used to support the goals and service plan.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Services and courses" icon="route" horizontal>
    Services, activities, courses of study, and outside agency connections that support the goals.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## Questions to ask

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What assessments informed the goals?">
    Ask whether the goals are based on current student information, not generic assumptions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What services support each goal?">
    Look for practical services, activities, instruction, related services, community experiences, or agency referrals tied to the goal.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Who owns each next step?">
    Ask which steps are the school's responsibility, which are family tasks, and which may involve outside agencies.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How will the team know the plan is working?">
    Ask what progress, participation, skill development, or follow-up will be reviewed before the next annual meeting.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Parent preparation

<Steps>
  <Step title="Bring strengths and interests">
    Write down what the student enjoys, avoids, talks about, practices, or wants to try.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Bring support needs">
    Note daily living, communication, transportation, employment, health, behavior, executive functioning, or community support needs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask about outside resources">
    Ask whether the student should connect with vocational rehabilitation, disability services, benefits planning, community programs, or other agencies.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Info>
  ISBE's parent resources point families to post-secondary transition resources, PUNS information, and statewide transition support. Start with the [ISBE parents and guardians information page](https://www.isbe.net/Pages/Special-Education-Parents-of-Students-with-Disabilities.aspx).
</Info>
