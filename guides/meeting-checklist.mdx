---
title: "What to Bring to an IEP Meeting"
description: "A practical checklist for preparing records, priorities, and questions."
---

Good preparation starts with a small set of useful records and a clear list of parent priorities. Do not try to bring every document in the house. Bring the records that explain the decision the team is about to make.

## Before the meeting

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm the purpose">
    Is this an annual review, eligibility meeting, reevaluation, amendment, transition meeting, behavior meeting, or problem-solving conversation?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the records">
    Pull the current plan, the most recent evaluation, progress reports, prior written notices, and recent communication about the concern.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write three priorities">
    Use this format: concern, record that shows it, question for the team.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Plan the follow-up">
    Decide what you need after the meeting: a revised plan, prior written notice, service clarification, evaluation plan, timeline, or written summary.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Records to gather

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Current plan" icon="file-text" horizontal>
    IEP, 504 plan, amendments, placement page, service page, and accommodation list.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Evaluation records" icon="clipboard-check" horizontal>
    Most recent evaluation, eligibility documents, outside reports, and parent input.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Progress records" icon="chart-no-axes-column" horizontal>
    Progress reports, report cards, work samples, service logs, behavior data, or attendance records.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Communication" icon="mails" horizontal>
    Meeting notices, prior written notices, recent emails, and notes from calls or informal conversations.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## Questions to keep nearby

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Where does this concern appear in the IEP or 504 plan?">
    If the concern is not reflected in the plan, ask whether the team needs more data, a new goal, an accommodation, a service change, or another next step.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What data is the team relying on?">
    Ask the team to identify the evaluation, progress data, observation, service log, work sample, or report that supports the recommendation.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Who is responsible for each next step?">
    Clarify who will do what, what will be documented, and when the family should expect follow-up.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Tip>
  ISBE points parents to a special education records keeper as a way to organize dates, people, meetings, reports, and paperwork over time. Use that idea even if you keep a simple folder or spreadsheet.
</Tip>
