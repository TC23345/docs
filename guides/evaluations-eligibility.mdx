---
title: "Evaluations and Eligibility"
description: "How to approach evaluation reports and eligibility conversations."
---

Evaluation reports can be long and technical. For a parent, the first goal is to understand what the school evaluated, what the results show, and how those results connect to support at school.

## Read the report in four passes

<Steps>
  <Step title="What was evaluated?">
    List the areas assessed, the tools used, and whether parent or teacher concerns were included.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What did the data show?">
    Look for patterns in academic, functional, communication, behavior, health, social, or adaptive needs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What did the team conclude?">
    Identify whether the report recommends eligibility, services, accommodations, further evaluation, or no special education eligibility.
  </Step>
  <Step title="What questions remain?">
    Mark areas that feel unsupported, missing, inconsistent, or hard to understand.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Useful questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Were all suspected areas of need evaluated?">
    If an area was not evaluated, ask how the team decided it was not needed and whether additional assessment should be considered.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do the results connect to school support?">
    Ask how the data connects to goals, services, accommodations, behavior supports, placement, or classroom access.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens if the team disagrees?">
    Ask what options exist for more discussion, additional data, parent input, independent evaluation questions, or dispute resolution information.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Eligibility meeting preparation

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Mark the findings" icon="highlighter" horizontal>
    Highlight the pages that explain the student's needs and the data behind those needs.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Mark the gaps" icon="circle-help" horizontal>
    Note areas that were not evaluated, do not match your observations, or need clearer explanation.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Bring parent input" icon="message-square-text" horizontal>
    Prepare concise examples from home, school communication, work completion, behavior, or daily functioning.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Ask for the next record" icon="file-plus" horizontal>
    If the team makes a decision, ask how it will be documented and when you will receive the written record.
  </Card>
</Columns>
