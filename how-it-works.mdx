The process is designed to lower the noise around a school concern. Each step turns scattered information into clearer records, parent priorities, meeting questions, and next steps.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Fit call">
    Start with a short conversation about the concern, the meeting date if one is scheduled, and what you are hoping to understand or change.

    **Decision point:** Is Shirley Road the right support, or should the family also contact an attorney, clinical provider, public agency, or parent center?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Records review">
    Relevant documents may include the current IEP or 504 plan, evaluation reports, progress reports, prior written notices, meeting notes, behavior plans, service logs, and district correspondence.

    **Decision point:** What do the records clearly show, and what still needs to be clarified?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Priorities and meeting plan">
    Before a meeting, the work becomes practical: identify the top priorities, organize the timeline, prepare questions, and decide what outcome would count as a useful next step.

    **Decision point:** What should the parent ask the school team to explain, decide, document, or revisit?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Meeting support">
    When appropriate, Shirley Road may support the family before and during an IEP or 504 meeting. The role is to help the parent stay organized, understand the conversation, and participate clearly.

    **Decision point:** What needs to be written into the plan, notice, or follow-up record?
  </Step>
  <Step title="Written next-step summary">
    After the meeting, families should know what happened, what is still open, what records or notices to watch for, and what follow-up may be useful.

    **Decision point:** What should happen next, who owns it, and when should the family follow up?
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What to send before a records review

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Core school records" icon="folder-open" horizontal>
    Current IEP or 504 plan, most recent evaluation, progress reports, and meeting notices.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Decision records" icon="file-text" horizontal>
    Prior written notices, eligibility documents, meeting notes, and placement or service decisions.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Concern records" icon="message-square-warning" horizontal>
    Emails, behavior notes, incident reports, attendance concerns, service logs, or work samples.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Parent priorities" icon="list-check" horizontal>
    Three questions you want answered and the outcome you hope the next meeting clarifies.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I need every document before reaching out?">
    No. Start with what you have. The first pass can identify what else may be useful to request or locate.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can Shirley Road attend every school meeting?">
    Meeting support depends on fit, timing, records, and role boundaries. Some situations are better handled through preparation only or through attorney involvement.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Will this guarantee a school decision?">
    No. Advocacy can improve preparation, clarity, and participation, but no advocate can guarantee a school outcome.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
  Shirley Road IEP Advocacy provides advocacy, preparation, and educational support for families. It does not provide legal advice, legal representation, clinical diagnosis, therapy services, or guaranteed school outcomes.
</Warning>
