Shirley Road offers practical advocacy support for families who need help preparing for school conversations, understanding records, and organizing next steps. The service language below is draft positioning for Rylee review before launch.

<Info>
  Most work starts with records. A useful advocacy plan usually depends on the current IEP or 504 plan, recent evaluations, progress data, notices, and the communication history around the concern.
</Info>

## Service menu

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="IEP and records review" icon="file-search">
    A focused review of the current IEP, evaluations, progress reports, prior written notices, and school communication.

    **Useful when:** the paperwork is hard to interpret or a meeting is coming up.

    **Output:** a plain-language summary of key issues, missing information, and questions to bring back to the team.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Meeting preparation" icon="calendar-check">
    Preparation for an IEP, 504, eligibility, reevaluation, transition, annual review, or problem-solving meeting.

    **Useful when:** the meeting matters but the parent priorities feel scattered.

    **Output:** a meeting plan with questions, records to reference, and next-step notes.
  </Card>
  <Card title="IEP or 504 meeting support" icon="users">
    Support before, during, and after a school meeting, within the role of an educational advocate.

    **Useful when:** parents want another prepared adult at the table.

    **Output:** preparation, meeting participation when appropriate, and follow-up summary.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Parent coaching" icon="messages-square">
    Short, focused support for parents who want to understand the process and communicate more clearly with the school team.

    **Useful when:** full advocacy support is not needed yet.

    **Output:** a practical action list and suggested language for emails or meetings.
  </Card>
  <Card title="School communication strategy" icon="mail-check">
    Help organizing timelines, concerns, and requests before communicating with a school team.

    **Useful when:** the story has become hard to track across emails, calls, and meetings.

    **Output:** a clean timeline, a priority list, and a suggested structure for the next communication.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Transition planning" icon="route">
    Support reading transition-related IEP sections and preparing questions about postsecondary goals, services, and planning.

    **Useful when:** the transition plan feels vague or disconnected from the student's strengths and support needs.

    **Output:** a parent-facing checklist of questions and records to review with the school team.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## What a records-first review looks for

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="IEP or 504 plan">
    - Present levels or current access needs.
    - Goals, accommodations, services, and placement language.
    - Progress reporting schedule.
    - Dates, responsible staff, and service frequency.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Evaluations">
    - Areas evaluated and areas not evaluated.
    - Parent and teacher concerns included in the report.
    - Data that supports eligibility, goals, services, or accommodations.
    - Questions that remain unanswered.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Communication">
    - Meeting notices and prior written notices.
    - Emails confirming requests, decisions, or timelines.
    - Notes from phone calls or informal conversations.
    - Follow-up items the team agreed to revisit.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Referral boundaries

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="When advocacy may be enough">
    Advocacy may be a fit when the parent needs help understanding records, preparing questions, organizing a meeting plan, or participating clearly in an IEP or 504 conversation.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When an attorney may be needed">
    Legal deadlines, due process, formal complaints, serious discipline or placement disputes, and questions about legal strategy should be reviewed with a qualified attorney.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When a clinical provider may be needed">
    Diagnosis, medical recommendations, therapy planning, or clinical treatment decisions belong with appropriately licensed clinical professionals.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Warning>
  Shirley Road IEP Advocacy provides advocacy, preparation, and educational support for families. It does not provide legal advice, legal representation, clinical diagnosis, therapy services, or guaranteed school outcomes.
</Warning>
